How often should fire extinguishers be serviced? The UK schedule

The 36th Company·7 min read·Updated Nov 2026

A fire extinguisher is only useful if it works on the day. In the UK that means a quick check every month, a full service by a competent person every year, and a deeper test every five or ten years depending on the type.

At a glance

Monthly: visual check by someone on site
Every year: basic service by a competent person
Every 5 years: extended service for water, foam, powder and wet chemical
Every 10 years: CO₂ and primary sealed powder
After any use: recharge or replace

The rules behind this
Fire Safety Order 2005 · Art. 17BS 5306-3BS 5306-8

The law and the standard behind it

The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires the responsible person to keep fire-fighting equipment in efficient working order and good repair (Article 17). The Order doesn't set a service interval for extinguishers. BS 5306-3 does: it is the code of practice for the commissioning and maintenance of portable extinguishers, and it is what fire and rescue services, insurers and service companies work to. Where a standard says "at least annually", assume an inspector will expect to see it done and recorded.

The four levels of maintenance

  1. Regular visual check (at least monthly). Done by someone on site who has been shown what to look for. Quick, and recorded.
  2. Basic service (every 12 months). Done by a competent person, usually a service engineer. A full examination of every extinguisher.
  3. Extended service (every 5 or 10 years). A deeper service that includes discharging and testing the extinguisher, checking it internally and refilling it.
  4. Overhaul or replacement. For CO₂ and some other types, a pressure test at 10 years. Many service providers also recommend replacing extinguishers at around 20 years old.

Extended service intervals by type

BAFE, the fire industry certification body, gives these intervals, based on BS 5306-3:

  • Water and water-based (including additive): basic service every year, extended service every 5 years.
  • Foam: basic service every year, extended service every 5 years.
  • Powder: basic service every year, extended service every 5 years.
  • Primary sealed powder: basic service every year, extended service every 10 years.
  • CO₂: basic service every year, with an extended service or pressure-test overhaul every 10 years.
  • Wet chemical: basic service every year, extended service every 5 years.

Your service company will keep track of each extinguisher's date of manufacture and last extended service. Ask them to list the due dates on your service report, so you can see what is coming up.

What to check yourself each month

The monthly check takes a couple of minutes per extinguisher. It is not a replacement for the engineer's annual service. It makes sure that nothing has gone wrong between visits. Walk the building and check that each extinguisher:

  • Is in its place, on a bracket or stand, and not moved or hidden behind stock, coats or a propped door.
  • Has a sign above it that is visible, and the right type of extinguisher for that location.
  • Has its safety pin and tamper seal in place.
  • Shows no dents, corrosion, leaks or damage to the body, hose or horn.
  • Has a pressure gauge, where fitted, reading in the green zone.
  • Has a clean, readable label facing outwards, and a service tag that is in date.
  • Has a hose and nozzle free of blockage, cracks or insect nests.

Record the date, the location and your initials, and report problems straight away. Our weekly, monthly and annual fire safety checks guide shows where this fits with the rest of your routine, and the fire safety log book guide shows how to record it.

What happens at the annual service

A competent engineer examines each extinguisher and checks things you can't see from the outside. Typically that covers the extinguisher's weight and pressure, the condition and operation of the valve, the hose, horn and seals, the contents and any signs of internal corrosion, and the mounting. They replace worn parts, fit a new tamper seal, attach a dated service label and give you a report listing each extinguisher, what was done, and anything that failed.

Use a service provider that is certified for this work, for example one registered under BAFE's scheme for extinguisher servicing, and ask for the certificate. The responsible person stays responsible for the result.

When to replace or recharge early

  • After any use, even a short burst. A partly discharged extinguisher can't be relied on. Have it recharged or replaced.
  • If the gauge is out of the green, the seal is broken or the pin is missing.
  • If it is damaged, dented or corroded, particularly the cylinder or valve.
  • If it fails its extended service, or is old enough that the engineer advises replacement.
  • If it is the wrong type for the risk. Your fire risk assessment may change which extinguishers you need. See which extinguisher for which fire.

Common mistakes

  • Servicing only when a salesperson calls, rather than on a fixed annual date.
  • Letting extended service dates slip because the annual visit "passed".
  • Not recording the monthly check, so there is no evidence it happened.
  • Moving extinguishers during refits or deliveries and not putting them back.
  • Keeping an old, fully coloured extinguisher that predates the current system. Our colour codes guide explains how to spot one.

Frequently asked questions

Is annual servicing a legal requirement?

The Fire Safety Order requires equipment to be maintained in efficient working order. It doesn't name a frequency. BS 5306-3 says at least annually, and fire services, insurers and risk assessors treat that as the standard. In practice, annual servicing is the expected evidence.

Can I service my own extinguishers?

Only if you are competent: you need training, the right equipment and the manufacturer's information, and extended services need workshop facilities. Most small businesses use a certified contractor.

Do extinguishers expire?

They don't have a single printed expiry date, but they do have a service life. Contents degrade, seals wear and cylinders corrode. That is why there are extended services and why replacement at around 20 years is commonly advised.

What about Ireland?

Ireland uses its own standard, I.S. 291, which was revised in 2015 and amended in 2022. It also requires servicing by a competent person every year.
