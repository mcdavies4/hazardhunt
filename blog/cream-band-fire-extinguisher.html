Cream band fire extinguisher: foam, and where it fits

The 36th Company·5 min read·Updated Oct 2026

A cream band marks a foam extinguisher, commonly AFFF (aqueous film-forming foam). It is the one for flammable liquids such as petrol and paint thinners, and many models also cover ordinary combustibles.

At a glance

Contains: foam, commonly AFFF
Fire classes: Class B (flammable liquids), and usually Class A; check the rating on the label
Not for: cooking oil and fat fires; fires involving electricity
Usually found: workshops, garages, plant rooms, anywhere flammable liquids are stored or used

The rules behind this
Fire Safety Order 2005 · Art. 13BS EN 3BS 5306-10:2019BS 5306-8

What a cream band means

Under the UK colour code, cream identifies a foam extinguisher. The body is red like every other extinguisher; the cream band or panel is what tells you the contents. It is easy to confuse with the red water band at a glance, which is one reason to learn the colours before an incident rather than during one.

How foam works

Foam is water with a foaming agent. It spreads over the surface of a burning liquid as a film or blanket, which cuts off the flammable vapour and the air. The water content also cools. That is why foam does a job on liquid fires that plain water cannot do.

What it is for

Foam is mainly chosen for Class B fires: petrol, oils, solvents and paint thinners. Many foam extinguishers are also rated for Class A fires such as wood, paper and fabric. Fire services take slightly different views on how good foam is on solids: Avon Fire & Rescue lists it for Classes A and B, while Hereford & Worcester advises it is less suited to Class A. The honest answer is to rely on the rating printed on your own extinguisher, not on the colour alone.

The technique for a liquid fire

Do not blast foam straight into a pool of burning liquid. That can splash the fuel and spread the fire. Fire service guidance is to aim at the inside edge of the container, or at a nearby surface, so the foam flows across the top of the liquid and settles as a layer.

Only fight a fire if it is small, you are trained, the alarm is raised and you have a clear exit behind you. Otherwise get out, stay out and call 999.

Never use it on

  • Cooking oil and fat fires (chip pans and fryers). These need wet chemical, as in our yellow band guide.
  • Fires involving live electrics. Foam is water-based, and Avon Fire & Rescue does not recommend it where electricity is involved. Use CO2 or follow your risk assessment.

Cream or red?

Both can tackle solids, but only foam handles flammable liquids. A workshop, garage or store with solvents and fuels points to foam. An office with mainly paper and furniture can work with water or foam, plus CO2 near electrical equipment. Our which extinguisher for which fire guide works through the choice by fuel.

Common mistakes

  • Spraying directly into the liquid rather than at the edge.
  • Treating a cream extinguisher as the all-rounder for a kitchen.
  • Using it on a computer or a plugged-in appliance fire.
  • Assuming every cream unit has the same ratings: models differ.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cream label extinguisher?

A foam extinguisher. The label or band colour is cream, and the contents are usually AFFF.

Can foam be used on electrical fires?

Fire services do not recommend it. Foam is water-based, so use a CO2 extinguisher or isolate the power if it is safe to do so.

Is foam better than water?

It covers flammable liquid fires that water cannot, and it is also rated for solids on most models. For ordinary combustibles only, water is a simple, effective choice.
