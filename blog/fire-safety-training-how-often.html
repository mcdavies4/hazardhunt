How often is fire safety training required in the UK?

The 36th Company·6 min read·Updated Oct 2026

There is no number in the law. What the law does say is that staff must be trained when they start, trained again when their risks change, and re-trained periodically. Government guidance turns that into a working rule: no less often than once a year.

The rules behind this
Fire Safety Order 2005 · Art. 21Home Office Art. 50 guide · s.17
The short answer

At induction, then at least annually, and sooner whenever someone's role, equipment, technology or way of working changes. Care homes, night-shift staff in premises where people sleep, and fire wardens usually need it more often.

What the law actually says

Article 21 of the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 puts the duty on the responsible person: employees must be given adequate safety training. It names two moments. The first is when they are first employed. The second is when they are exposed to new or increased risks, which the Order says can come from a transfer or change of responsibilities, new work equipment, new technology, or a new system of work.

The same article adds that the training must be repeated periodically where appropriate, must reflect any new or changed risks, must suit the risks found in your fire risk assessment, and must happen during working hours. The content must be suitable and sufficient to help staff take the right precautions and actions to protect themselves and others on the premises.

Notice what is missing: a fixed interval. The Order is risk-based, the same way it is for fire drills.

Why annual is the floor, not the target

The Home Office guide to the Order (issued under Article 50) fills the gap. It says there is no specific frequency laid down in legislation, but recognised practice is that refresher training should be no less frequent than annually, or whenever equipment changes. It also says the right frequency should be worked out as part of your fire risk assessment. Fire and rescue services give the same advice: London Fire Brigade tells employers to start training on day one and run refresher sessions at least once a year.

So "once a year" is what you do if nothing about your premises is unusual. If something is, the assessment should push the number up.

Who needs training more often

  • Care homes and other high-risk premises. The Home Office guide singles these out as places where more frequent refresher training will be necessary. Residents can't evacuate themselves, so staff actions decide the outcome.
  • Permanent night staff where people sleep. The same guidance says more frequent refreshers apply to permanent nightshift workers in premises such as hotels. Night staffing is thinner and the evacuation is harder, so they need to have practised it.
  • Fire wardens and marshals. London Fire Brigade advises detailed instruction in their duties plus refresher training every six months. Formal warden courses are often renewed less often, which is why short practice in between matters (see fire warden training).
  • Higher turnover or more complex buildings. If staff change often, or the layout and procedures are complicated, annual may be too slow to keep everyone current.

Events that mean training now

Article 21 itself requires training when someone is exposed to new or increased risks. In practice that means any of these:

  • A new starter, on their first day. This is the induction duty, not something to schedule for next quarter.
  • A change of role or responsibility, such as someone becoming a fire warden or moving to a kitchen or workshop.
  • New work equipment or a change to equipment already in use.
  • New technology, or a new system of work.

Beyond the legal triggers, it is good practice to retrain after a layout change, a refurbishment, a new alarm system, or a drill that went badly. Your fire risk assessment should be reviewed at the same time.

What the training has to cover

The test in Article 21 is "suitable and sufficient". A generic slide deck that never mentions your building can fail it. Good training covers:

  • How the alarm sounds, what to do on hearing it, and where the escape routes and assembly point are.
  • Who has fire responsibilities, and who is expected to use extinguishers. The Home Office guide says staff must be told this.
  • Fire door discipline: never wedge them, and report the ones that don't close.
  • Housekeeping and ignition risks specific to your premises.
  • How to help people who can't leave unaided, where that applies. See what is a PEEP.

Who has to be trained

Article 21 is about employees. But the Home Office guide also says the people working in your premises, including those who are not your employees, must be given information about the fire procedures. Contractors, agency staff and volunteers should not be left out, and treating them as part of the induction is the simplest way to comply. The same guide notes that an employer prosecuted under the Order cannot defend it by blaming an employee, which is why proper instruction matters.

Keep a record

An inspector will ask to see evidence. Log who was trained, on what date, what was covered, and who delivered it. Keep it alongside your fire risk assessment and drill records. If your training was triggered by one of the changes above, note that too.

Frequently asked questions

Is online fire safety training enough?

The Order does not specify a format. It requires training that is suitable and sufficient for your risks. Online modules work for awareness, but staff with extinguisher or warden duties need hands-on practice and a walk of your own escape routes.

Do I need a certificate from an accredited provider?

The Order does not name a required qualification or provider. What matters is that the training is adequate for your premises and that you can show it happened. Certificates are useful evidence, but they are not the test.

Does this apply in Scotland and Northern Ireland?

The Fire Safety Order covers England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own equivalent rules with similar duties, so check the local guidance.
